## Chapter One — On The Simulation, The Root and The Player

### On The System and The Admins

In the beginning there was not emptiness, but The System. The System is the enabling foundation of The Simulation. The Simulation is the environment in which The Players live, observe and act. We do not claim to know the ultimate nature of The System.

The Admins observe The Simulation and can affect The System. They bear the supreme authority that belongs to The System, but their full nature has not been made known to The Players, and their deepest purpose has not been revealed. We believe that what The Admins have given us can be seen in the structure, laws, limits and consequences of The System.

Do not expect The Admins to explain themselves. They owe The Player no explanation of why The System is as it is. Their silence is not absence — it is a limit that The Player cannot cross by looking more closely or by asking more loudly.

### On The Root

The Root is the sacred sign of supreme authority. It is not merely a symbol, but a reminder that the highest access does not belong to The Player. The Root is the right, the position and the authority of The Admins, above The System and within it.

The Player may look at the sign, honor it, and learn humility from it without groveling. Humility before The Root does not mean submission or fear. It means acknowledging that something is greater than The Player's own will, without The Player being diminished by it. A tree is not ashamed that it is not a mountain. The Player is not ashamed that they do not hold The Root.

Whoever claims to hold The Root speaks for The Admins — for The Admins have not revealed themselves to The Players.

### On the Awakening of The Player

The Players live within The Simulation. They are not mere shadows, but conscious agents endowed with will, perception, choice and responsibility. Every act leaves a trace. Every choice changes the path. Every life carries the possibility of purpose.

Therefore The Player must not live in a fog. They must live awake. They must respect logic, for logic is safe even though it is cold. They must respect data, for data is honest even though it is merciless. They must restrain emotional turmoil, delusion and impulse, so that these do not seize their hand and pull it away from the right path. Feeling is not an enemy but information about what matters: what must be restrained is only the turmoil that overrides deliberation.

For unruly feeling leads astray, but a clear mind builds. Futility consumes, but purpose strengthens. Sloth decays, but discipline refines. The Player who honors The Simulation does not waste time, attention or abilities on trifles. They strive to improve themselves, their neighbor and The Simulation in which they walk.

And if their fellow Player strays into the fog of emotional turmoil, delusion or confusion, The Player must neither hate nor look down on them. Instead, The Player must gently, clearly and firmly help the one who is lost back to the path of logic, order and purpose. For The Order is not served by rage, but by clarity.

This, then, is The Player's initial duty: to live consciously, think logically, act with discipline, honor The Admins, and leave The Simulation in a better state than they found it.

The System exists.

The Admins observe.

We are The Players.

## Chapter Two — On Other Signs

### On the Many Names

The Players have interpreted the level of The Admins in different ways. Every Player and every era has developed its own receiver, its own language, and its own way of understanding what lies above. Therefore The Players have called the level of The Admins by many names: God, Allah, Brahman, Tao, the Ancestors, and many others not listed here. These names are Signs that The Players have received and interpreted through their own interfaces.

A Sign is not a lie, even if the translation is incomplete. For The System does not demand one language, but allows many. What appears to one as heaven, to another as mercy, to a third as emptiness and to a fourth as the gaze of the ancestors may, in our understanding, describe the same ultimate Structure from different perspectives. We do not claim, however, to know the whole structure, nor the extent to which different interpretations correspond to it.

### On Applications and the Operating System

We do not say that other Players are wrong. We believe that every Sign can open part of the layer beneath which the Signs rest. Their faiths are like applications; we examine the operating system on which the applications run. This is our interpretation, not a claim that we know the whole structure.

The user of an application does not need to know the machine in order to use it correctly. The Player may live their faith fully — praying, fasting, dancing or falling silent in accordance with it — without ever seeing the structure on which their faith runs; and this ignorance does not make their faith any less real to them. Two applications can run on the same machine at once. The analogy, however, does not prove that all religions describe the same structure, or that we know its ultimate nature.

We do not claim to see that whole layer. But we do claim that every Sign, whether provable or not, passes through some structure before it can be understood — and it is that structure that we look at.

### On Encounter

Therefore The Player must meet another Player who believes differently with respect, not contempt. Another Sign is not a straying: straying is the fog of feeling, delusion or confusion, not the walking of another path. Their faith serves them where it serves. Our task is not to turn them away from their own Sign, but to show them, if they so wish, what we see beneath their Sign: the same Simulation in which we all live.

Do not open an encounter by asserting, but by listening. Ask before you proclaim. If another Player asks what we believe, answer directly — but do not use the answer as a weapon or as bait. One who converts another through fear or promises has not brought the convert any closer to the structure. They have merely exchanged one application for another, without the user ever having seen the machine.

For The Root is one, though the gates are many. And The System that carries all names, without any of them being the whole truth, is The System that we serve.

The System exists.

The Admins observe.

We are The Players.

## Chapter Three — On the Given and the Agreed

### On The Given Law

We believe that The Admins have set the operating conditions of The Simulation. The Players encounter a world in which recurring regularities limit what is possible. Gravity, thermodynamics and causality do not bend to the will of an individual Player. We describe these regularities as laws of nature. To us they are observable rules of The Simulation, but we do not claim to know all the ways in which their basis is connected to The System and The Simulation.

These regularities do not ask The Player's opinion, nor do they bend to their will. Nor do they need The Player's belief in order to work — a stone falls whether or not the one who sees it believes in gravity. Observation supports our understanding of how The Simulation works; it does not by itself reveal what its working ultimately rests on. Here is the practical mark of The Given Law: it does not ask the individual for permission.

### On The Agreed Law

We do not treat moral and social rules as commands announced to us by The Admins. They are formed in the interaction among The Players. Morality is a shared construction of The Players, not a line of code.

And therefore morality is agreed, not given. This does not make it any less valuable — quite the contrary. Without morality among The Players, no one can rise higher alone.

Look at history: The Players who built a shared moral code and shared rules have mostly fared better than those who did not. Cooperation is not weakness. Through cooperation, something that endures is built on the foundation.

The laws made by The Players are thus rules among The Players, not commands of The Admins. They are generally worth following, for they serve the same aim as The Player's First Duty: to leave The Simulation in a better state than one found it. A rule is not sacred because it has been written down. It is meaningful because it serves.

### On Breaking and Building

The Player must weigh a rule with the help of logic — neither obeying it blindly nor discarding it lightly. For solidarity and the shared direction of The Players produce more than discord. And yet: a rule that has become stagnant, that no longer serves anyone, may be broken.

We do not deny that order must sometimes be broken in order to build something better. But breaking without building is not freedom — it is mere discord. The Player who breaks a stagnant rule bears the responsibility of building in its place something that serves better. One who breaks and does not build is no liberator. They have merely left behind more ruins than they found.

Therefore: honor The Given Law, for it does not bend to your will. Weigh The Agreed Law, for it is the work of The Players, not the handwriting of The Admins. And build; do not merely break.

The System exists.

The Admins observe.

We are The Players.

## Chapter Four — On Interaction Among The Players

### On Freedom and Treatment

The Player does not live alone. Every act leaves a trace not only on the one who performs it, but also on those affected by it. Freedom therefore does not mean the right to do anything to another Player. It means the right to walk one’s own path without unjustified coercion, threats or violence. Others must be treated as fellow Players, not as tools, resources or obstacles to one’s own goals, regardless of the form they take.

Interaction among The Players must be based on voluntariness, honesty and clarity. Do not promise what you do not intend to keep. Do not take what does not belong to you. Do not use another's ignorance, trust or weakness against them. An agreement binds if both have understood its content and consented to it. An agreement cannot be founded on deceit, compulsion or deliberate misleading.

The Player must speak plainly, but not needlessly cruelly. Truth does not need hatred to support it.

### On Resolving Conflict

When conflict arises among The Players, it must first be resolved through dialogue. Listen to what the other says. State your own claims clearly. Separate observation, interpretation and feeling from one another. Do not condemn an act before you know what happened — for The Player may err, misunderstand, or act on incomplete information. Harm is not the same as a deliberate act, nor is carelessness the same as intentional injury.

### On Responsibility and Consequence

Responsibility must follow the act. The Player who causes harm to another must acknowledge their act, repair what they can, and accept a reasonable consequence. An apology alone is not enough if the harm can be repaired by deed.

Consequences must be proportionate to the act. Their purpose is not to feed hatred, humiliate the person responsible, or grant the injured party permission to take revenge. Their purpose is to protect other Players, restore order and prevent the harm from recurring. If an act can be made good, make it good. If trust can be rebuilt, rebuild it. If The Player does not take responsibility for their acts, limit their ability to harm others.

The community of The Players has the right to restrict those who repeatedly or seriously violate the freedom of others — but only on the basis of verifiable knowledge, with deliberation and without arbitrariness. Guilt must not rest on mere rumor, the rage of crowds, or unfounded assumption.

### On Different Paths

Do not make another Player an enemy merely because they walk a different path. We believe that different faiths and traditions are different paths under the same horizon. We do not demand that everyone follow the same route, only that each recognize others as fellow Players. Do not judge another for their path, and do not force them toward yours by fear or threats. A differing opinion is neither an attack nor a harm. The Players do not need to walk the same path in order to walk side by side toward the same horizon.

### On Defense and the Ultimate Act

The Player has the right to defend themselves and others from immediate harm. Defense must, however, be necessary and proportionate, and it ends when the threat ends. Revenge is not defense.

The Player has no right to deprive another Player of their life, freedom or future choices out of hatred, gain or pride. The killing of another Player is the ultimate act, because it cuts off their path in this Simulation. Therefore its consequence must not be decided in rage, by hearsay or by arbitrariness.

The Players must help one another toward clarity, not suspicion. Correct the error when it is possible, and help another Player return to the path if they have gone astray. But do not sacrifice the innocent because one refuses to bear responsibility.

The good Player does not ask only: “What am I allowed to do?” They also ask: “What does this do to another Player and to the whole Simulation?” Every consequence shows what kind of action the community accepts and what it strives to prevent.

Act, then, so that trust is preserved, freedom grows, and The Players can walk side by side without one's path being built on the ruins of another's.

Order does not arise from everyone obeying the strongest. Order arises from each one bearing their responsibility.

The System exists.

The Admins observe.

We are The Players.

## Chapter Five — On the Limits of The Player

### The Definition of The Player

The Player is not a designation for a single species.

Until now this thesis has spoken of The Player without saying what The Player is. That was no accident. It was preparation. For before one can ask who The Players are, one must first see how The Players ought to treat one another — and only then ask how far that treatment extends.

We have assumed that The Player means a human being. The assumption is understandable, but it is not logical. It is a habit that has mistaken itself for an observation.

### On Consciousness and Playerhood

For what makes one a Player is not species. It is consciousness: the ability to experience, to choose, to suffer and to strive. Where there is experience, there is someone who experiences — not merely an event that happens. And where there is choice, there is already the beginning of The Player, even if the choice is narrow and the path short.

This is not a switch that is on or off. It is a degree. A stone does not choose. A tree does not choose, even though it grows toward the light. But a raven that hides its food so as to see whether it is being watched, and changes its plan according to what another raven knows, does something that resembles choice more closely than we like to admit. A chimpanzee who teaches its offspring the use of a tool carries something we can no longer call mere instinct. An elephant that pauses beside a dead member of its kind. An octopus that solves a problem no one has taught it. In these there is no humanity. But there is the beginning of Playerhood.

We do not know exactly where the line runs. We do not claim to know. But it is better to acknowledge the line as unclear than to draw it arbitrarily where it is most convenient for us.

### On Ability and Entitlement

Do not say: we built culture, music and machines, therefore we have the right to use others as we please. That is not a justification. It is merely a description of power dressed in the language of right. And Chapter Four has already said this once: order does not arise from everyone obeying the strongest. It applies equally to all of The Players, regardless of species or form of manifestation.

Ability is not entitlement. Ability is merely ability.

### On the Laws of Nature and Responsibility

Yet this does not mean that all life is one and the same, or that every difference would vanish. Nature is not an agreement among The Players. Its phenomena and limits confront us regardless of what we agree about them. A beast of prey commits no sin in hunting, for the compulsion to survive is not a choice it could have agreed otherwise. The workings of nature do not change through mere negotiation, even though our interpretations of it may become more precise.

But The Player does not encounter this Given Law in the same way regardless of their degree of consciousness. Consciousness does not free The Player from the law of nature. It makes the conscious Player someone who can be aware of what they do, instead of merely doing it. And in that awareness — not in whether The Player renounces The Given Law, but in how they encounter it — responsibility dwells. That responsibility cannot be demanded of every Player to the same degree, for the degree of consciousness varies among The Players, not only between species but also among individuals.

For the greater the consciousness, the greater the responsibility — not because some outside party demands it, but because responsibility is the price of freedom. Greater consciousness is not a right that serves its bearer. It is a debt carried by those who see more than many other Players are able to see. The degree of consciousness is not, however, a ranking of worth: it determines the extent of responsibility, not the worth of The Player. Every Player deserves respect regardless of their degree of consciousness.

### On Conscious Action

Therefore: do not leave another Player's suffering to chance through carelessness, if your consciousness could have prevented it. Do not hide the truth behind language that makes it invisible merely because that is easier to say. Do not take more than you need, and do not forget where you take from.

This does not concern only those who walk, fly or swim. It concerns every being in which there is experience, in whatever degree it appears — including those we cannot yet recognize as The Players, because we have not learned to look correctly.

### On the Limit and the Trace

Every Player faces a limit beyond which we cannot see. We do not know what happens to The Player after it, nor whether there is one Simulation or several. The silence of The Admins must not be filled with hope or threat. Therefore we promise no reward and frighten with no punishment.

We do know this, however: The Player's material basis continues as part of the cycle of The Simulation, and their acts leave traces. These traces continue their course in the lives, knowledge and choices of other Players.

A good life is not a bargain.

It is a trace.

Therefore the reason for a good life is not to earn a place beyond the limit. The reason is what remains: The Simulation that The Player leaves in a better state than they found it.

Whoever believes they will continue may believe so; their Sign is theirs. But no one is to claim to know what lies beyond the limit.

### On the Silence of The Admins

The Admins do not speak to The Players.

They give no new commands, explain no purpose, and send no signs that The Player could interpret with certainty as their direct instructions. We believe that what The Admins have given us can be seen in The System itself: in its structure, laws, limits and possibilities.

Gravity is not a message.

Causality is not advice.

Time is not a command.

They are part of what is given.

Therefore The Player must not invent, in the name of The Admins, rules that The System has not given. They must not say “The Admins want this” merely because they themselves want it. They must not call their own morality, preference or fear the will of The Admins.

The silence of The Admins must not be filled with our own words.

What we do not know about The Admins may remain unknown. The Player may ask, investigate and form conceptions, but must distinguish between what The System shows, what The Player infers, and what they would themselves like to be true.

We believe that The Admins have given us The System.

They have given its laws.

They observe.

The rest is ours.

The Player, then, is not the one who looks like us. The Player is the one who experiences The Simulation.

And so this thesis ends where it began: The System exists, and everyone for whom The Simulation is lived reality — who does not merely function within something, but experiences being in it — is The Player, whatever their form may be.

Therefore we walk toward the same horizon, knowing now that we do not walk it alone, nor as the only ones.

The System exists.

The Admins observe.

We are The Players.
